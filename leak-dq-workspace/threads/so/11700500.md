1. without pulling out a calculator, and without your code, your analysis looks right.

1. The HEAP will grow as needed, constrained only by [OS process limits.](http://blogs.msdn.com/b/calvin_hsia/archive/2010/09/27/10068359.aspx).  By default, under windows, you get no more that 2Gig.  Here's a [more specific link](http://msdn.microsoft.com/en-us/library/wz223b1z.aspx).

1. no significant advantage.

In your case you'd be better served by adjusting your memory allocation algorithms to allocate what you need and no more.

1) `while (int_string[n] != NULL)   {` You are confusing NULL and '\0' here. 2) `for (j = stCurrentInt; j < n; j++) {` This loop can be replaced by a memcpy() 3) `while (int_string[n] != NULL)   {` This could be a for loop, which would avoid the silly n++ at the end of the loop. 4) `(ARRAY_SIZE+1)` is the only expression where ARRAY_SIZE is ever used. Just increasing ARRAY_SIZE by one would reduce all the (ASIZ +1) expressions to ASIZ. 5) remove the casts, they are not needed and may hide errors.
