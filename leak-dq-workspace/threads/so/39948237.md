In general, the caller still owns the reference and must decrement it. Callables that steal a reference should document that they do. So for instance [PyTuple_SetItem](https://docs.python.org/3/c-api/tuple.html#c.PyTuple_SetItem) notes that it steals a reference. It is a bit confusing and perhaps risky but I guess if 99% of the functions act one way, its a bit tedious to keep mentioning.

@Petr: For these sorts of questions, it's also frequently helpful to look at Python's own source. There are plenty of occurrences of `PyObject_CallObject` in the `Modules` subdirectory of the source tree. Note that most of those occurrences *don't* do a `Py_DECREF` on the `PyObject_CallObject` arguments, but that's usually because they're passing references that they don't own in the first place (e.g., because those references were in turn borrowed from the caller of the function that calls `PyObject_CallObject`).
